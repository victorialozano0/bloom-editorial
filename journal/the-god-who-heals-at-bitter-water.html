The God Who Heals at Bitter Water

God named Himself Healer at bitter water. For the woman tired of asking, there is still a hem to reach for.

By Hannah Grace · 3 min read

God named Himself Healer at bitter water.

Three days after the Red Sea, the singing stopped.

Israel had just watched God part the water. Miriam had danced with her tambourine. Then they walked into the desert, found water at last, and it was bitter. Undrinkable. The place was called Marah, which means exactly that: bitter.

The people who had been singing turned on Moses. Moses cried out to God. God showed him a piece of wood, Moses threw it into the water, and the water became sweet. And right there, with the complaint still hanging in the desert air, God gives Himself a name:

"I am the Lord who heals you." (Exodus 15:26, NIV)

A name spoken in the desert

Notice where God says it. Not after the desert. Not after everyone has calmed down and learned their lesson. He speaks this name to people who are still thirsty, still afraid, still wondering if He brought them out here to let them die.

Healing is not a reward for having finished suffering. It is a name God wears into the middle of it.

If you have prayed for healing for a long time, you may know a quieter ache: the embarrassment of asking again. The sense that you've bothered God with the same request for so long that you must be doing something wrong.

I used to read healing promises like a vending machine. Insert enough faith, receive the outcome. When the outcome didn't come, I blamed my faith, or decided God had reasons I wasn't holy enough to understand. Every one of those conclusions made God smaller and my suffering larger.

The woman who reached anyway

Mark tells us about a woman who had been bleeding for twelve years. Under the law it left her unclean, cut off from touch, from worship, from the life of her community. And she had tried everything. "She had suffered a great deal under the care of many doctors and had spent all she had, yet instead of getting better she grew worse." (Mark 5:26, NIV)

She didn't push to the front. She didn't ask for an appointment. She reached through the crowd from behind, still hiding, and touched the hem of His garment.

And Jesus stopped. He looked for her. When she came forward trembling and told Him everything, He said: "Daughter, your faith has healed you. Go in peace and be freed from your suffering." (Mark 5:34, NIV)

Daughter. She expected exposure, and she received a name. Seen. Known. Belonged.

She didn't feel healed before she reached. She reached in desperation, not certainty. Some days, the prayer is just the reaching.

A practice for the flat days

This week, when prayer feels like it's hitting a ceiling, don't stop praying. Name the absence to God out loud: "Lord, I don't feel You in this right now. I'm naming that honestly. I'm choosing to stay at Marah anyway."

Then read this slowly, once: "He heals the brokenhearted and binds up their wounds." (Psalm 147:3, NIV)

In your journal, write one honest sentence in answer to this question: Where might healing already be happening that I haven't been watching for?
