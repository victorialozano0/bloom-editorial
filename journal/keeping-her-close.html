Keeping Her Close: Recipes, Handwriting, and Unsent Letters

Her recipe cards, her handwriting, a letter that begins Dear Mom: small, quiet ways to keep your mother near.

By Clara Bennett · 2 min read

Write to her anyway.

There is a drawer somewhere with her handwriting in it. A grocery list. A birthday card. A recipe card with a stain in the corner, from the year she made that dish for everyone.

You may not open the drawer often. Some days you cannot. Some days you open it just to see the way she wrote her capital letters.

That counts, too.

Her recipes, her hands, her kitchen

So much of a mother lives in the small, repeated things. The way she cut onions. The pan she always used. The note in the margin that says less salt or double this, in ink that has started to fade.

Cooking one of her recipes can feel like standing next to her. It can also feel unbearable. You might make it once and cry the whole time. You might not be ready to make it at all. There is no right time and no wrong one.

If you do cook it, you do not have to make it perfectly. Hers was never written down all the way anyway. Some of it was only in her hands.

A letter you never send is still a letter. She is still the one it is for.

Her handwriting is its own kind of keepsake. Some daughters frame a single line. Some tuck a card into the book they read at night. Some just keep it close, in a wallet or a jacket pocket, where it can be touched without being looked at.

Writing to her anyway

And then there are the things you never got to say. Or the things you said a hundred times and want to say once more.

A letter you never send is still a letter. She is still the one it is for. You can start with Dear Mom and write one sentence, or ten pages. You can tell her about your week. You can tell her you are angry. You can tell her you miss the way she hummed while she folded towels.

None of this is about moving on. It is about keeping a door open. Her recipes, her handwriting, your letters to her: these are small ways of saying she is still part of your life, because she is.

You do not need a ritual that looks a certain way. You only need something that feels like her.

A gentle prompt: Begin a letter with "Dear Mom," and tell her about one small thing that happened today. Something she would have wanted to hear about.

Clara's journals, including a recipe memory book and a book of letters to Mom, are on the way.
