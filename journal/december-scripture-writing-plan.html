December Scripture Writing Plan: Peace for an Anxious Heart (KJV)

A December scripture writing plan from the KJV: eight passages on peace, two a week, with rest days and one ten-minute practice.

By Hannah Grace · 8 min read

Eight passages of peace for December.

It is the first week of December, and the kitchen table already holds a stack of cards, a school form and a list that grew overnight. The house is still dark. You have ten minutes before anyone else is awake, and your mind has already left for the twentieth.

A scripture writing plan is made for those ten minutes. It asks one thing of you: copy a short passage of the Bible by hand, slowly, and stay with it for a few days. This December scripture writing plan uses eight passages about peace from the King James Version, two a week, with rest days built in.

Begin with what it cannot do. Writing a verse will not finish the list or lighten a hard month, and nobody can promise that it will quiet an anxious heart. What it gives you is smaller and still worth having: one true sentence, and ten minutes in which your hand and your attention are in the same place.

How a scripture writing plan works: write, reflect, remember

Copying Scripture is an old habit. In Deuteronomy, the king of Israel was told to write out his own copy of the law and read it all the days of his life (Deuteronomy 17:18-19). The psalmist says why in one line: "Thy word have I hid in mine heart, that I might not sin against thee." (Psalm 119:11, KJV)

Writing by hand slows you down. Your hand has to follow every word, including the small ones you have read past for years. You cannot skim a sentence you are writing by hand.

You cannot skim a sentence you are writing by hand.

The journal this plan comes from gives every passage two pages, one to write it out and one to reflect. On some of the reflection pages you write the passage again from memory. For December, I have spread that work across three short days:

  • Write. Copy the passage word for word, thee and thou included.
  • Reflect. Read your copy and answer one question about it in a few honest sentences.
  • Remember. Write it again from memory. Peek if you need to. Memory grows by trying.

The December plan: eight passages, four weeks

This part of the journal has eight passages, so the plan has eight, in the same order. I have not added verses to fill thirty-one days. Each passage gets three days, every seventh day is rest, and the last three days of the month are for looking back. The list gives the first words of each passage. Copy the whole verse from your Bible.

Week 1: John 14:27 and Isaiah 26:3

  • December 1: Write John 14:27. "Peace I leave with you, my peace I give unto you..." (KJV)
  • December 2: Reflect on John 14:27.
  • December 3: Remember. Write John 14:27 again from memory.
  • December 4: Write Isaiah 26:3. "Thou wilt keep him in perfect peace..." (KJV)
  • December 5: Reflect on Isaiah 26:3.
  • December 6: Remember. Write Isaiah 26:3 again from memory.
  • December 7: Rest.

Week 2: 1 Peter 5:7 and Psalm 94:19

  • December 8: Write 1 Peter 5:7. "Casting all your care upon him..." (KJV)
  • December 9: Reflect on 1 Peter 5:7.
  • December 10: Remember. Write 1 Peter 5:7 again from memory.
  • December 11: Write Psalm 94:19. "In the multitude of my thoughts within me..." (KJV)
  • December 12: Reflect on Psalm 94:19.
  • December 13: Remember. Write Psalm 94:19 again from memory.
  • December 14: Rest.

Week 3: Matthew 6:34 and Psalm 56:3

  • December 15: Write Matthew 6:34. "Take therefore no thought for the morrow..." (KJV)
  • December 16: Reflect on Matthew 6:34.
  • December 17: Remember. Write Matthew 6:34 again from memory.
  • December 18: Write Psalm 56:3. "What time I am afraid, I will trust in thee." (KJV)
  • December 19: Reflect on Psalm 56:3.
  • December 20: Remember. Write Psalm 56:3 again from memory.
  • December 21: Rest.

Week 4: 2 Thessalonians 3:16 and Psalm 55:22

  • December 22: Write 2 Thessalonians 3:16. "Now the Lord of peace himself give you peace always by all means..." (KJV)
  • December 23: Reflect on 2 Thessalonians 3:16.
  • December 24: Remember. Write 2 Thessalonians 3:16 again from memory.
  • December 25: Write Psalm 55:22. "Cast thy burden upon the LORD, and he shall sustain thee..." (KJV)
  • December 26: Reflect on Psalm 55:22.
  • December 27: Remember. Write Psalm 55:22 again from memory.
  • December 28: Rest.

The last three days: looking back

  • December 29: Look back over the eight passages.
  • December 30: Choose the one passage you want to keep.
  • December 31: Write it once more.

The dates are a guide. Start late if you need to, and keep the order. If you miss a day, skip it and keep going, because nothing resets. On a day with only five minutes, just write the verse. The reflection can wait.

The third week holds the one creative practice in this part of the journal, a breath prayer on Psalm 56:3. Breathe at your ordinary pace, with no holding and no counting, and stop if it feels uncomfortable. Say the first half of the verse as you breathe in, "What time I am afraid," and the second half as you breathe out, "I will trust in thee." (Psalm 56:3, KJV) Then write two short lists: what you are afraid of today, and what you will trust Him with.

One day, start to finish

Here is December 1 in full. Every "write" day follows the same shape. You need a King James Bible, a pen, and a notebook or a plain index card. Give it about ten minutes. The journal sets no timer.

The passage: "Peace I leave with you, my peace I give unto you: not as the world giveth, give I unto you. Let not your heart be troubled, neither let it be afraid." (John 14:27, KJV)

  1. Sit down before you open anything else. Phone face down, in another room if you can.
  2. Read the passage aloud once. Go slowly enough to hear the pause in the middle.
  3. Copy it word for word. Keep the old verb endings and the punctuation.
  4. Underline the word or phrase that stopped you. Choose one. It might be "my peace." It might be "afraid."
  5. Read your own handwriting back. Once, silently, as if someone had left it on the table for you.
  6. Close with one sentence of thanks. A small one counts. Then shut the notebook.

On December 2, the reflect day, open to your copy and answer the question the journal asks of this verse: how is the peace Jesus gives different from the peace the world offers me? For the other passages, two plain questions will serve. What does this show me about God? Where do I need this right now? On December 3, turn to a clean page and write the verse from memory.

In Scripture Journal for Women, each of these eight passages is printed at the top of a lined page, followed by a reflection page with a question written for that passage. The breath prayer on Psalm 56:3 has a practice page of its own, and the part closes with a Looking Back page.

What you might notice

You might notice a word you have read past for years. You might notice nothing. Nothing is an honest result, and the verse is on the page either way.

Some of these passages touch fear and weariness, and December can press on both. If writing stirs up more than you can carry, put the pen down, drink a glass of water and talk to someone you trust. If anxiety is with you most days, it deserves the help of a doctor or a counselor as well as your prayers. Asking for that help is no failure of faith.

If what surfaces is an old grief more than a present worry, you may need different words first. How to lament walks through the four movements of Lamentations 3. If this is a December of waiting on a body, there are healing scriptures for the woman tired of asking that you can write out the same way.

When to use this scripture writing plan

  • Early mornings. Before the phone, while the house is dark.
  • Advent. If you keep Advent, the four weeks sit beside it easily. The plan reaches Christmas Eve with "Now the Lord of peace himself give you peace always by all means. The Lord be with you all." (2 Thessalonians 3:16, KJV)
  • A hard December. The first one after a loss, a month of money worry, a week of waiting on test results. Two passages a week is small enough to keep when little else is.

Questions women ask about a scripture writing plan

What is a scripture writing plan? It is a list of short Bible passages to copy by hand, slowly, on set days. This one has eight passages about peace from the King James Version, two a week through December, with rest days built in.

How do I start scripture writing? For this plan you need a King James Bible, a pen, and a notebook or a plain index card. Read the passage aloud once, copy it word for word, and underline the word or phrase that stopped you. About ten minutes is enough.

How many verses should I write a day? One passage. In this plan the same passage stays with you for three days, to write, reflect and remember. On a day with only five minutes, just write the verse.

The journal holds 96 passages in twelve parts, and these eight make up one part. A notebook will do the same work this month.

When December ends, the 2027 scripture writing plan sets all twelve parts across the year, one theme a month.

The list on the kitchen table will still be there when the ten minutes are over. So will the verse, in your own handwriting.
